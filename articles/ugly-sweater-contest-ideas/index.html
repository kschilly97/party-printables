Ugly Sweater Contest Ideas: Categories, Judging and Fun Twists

Updated October 14, 2026

An ugly sweater contest can be the highlight of a holiday party — or a five-minute moment nobody notices. The difference is in how you run it. A little structure, creative categories and a moment in the spotlight for each contestant turn a dress code into an event.

Start with the invitation

Make the theme clear and encourage effort. Mention the contest and the categories on the invitation so people know they can win for creativity, not just for having the gaudiest sweater. Point out that DIY sweaters are welcome; it encourages people who don't own one to join in.

Award categories beyond "Ugliest"

Having only one prize means most people don't stand a chance. Multiple categories give more people a shot and make the awards segment more fun.

  • Ugliest Overall — the classic.
  • Most Creative DIY — hand-made, glued, sewn or stapled.
  • Best Use of Lights — anything that blinks, glows or requires batteries.
  • Most 3D — pom-poms, stuffed animals, bells, tinsel.
  • Best Pun — wordplay sweaters.
  • Most Likely to Be Worn Again — the sweater that's secretly great.
  • Best Couple or Group Theme — matching or coordinated sweaters.
  • Best Accessories — headbands, earrings, socks, scarves.
  • Most Festive Pet — if pets are attending.
  • Honorable Mention: Tried Their Best — for the sweater that's barely ugly.

Judging methods

Popular vote

Hand out ballots and have everyone vote by category. Count at the end. Fair and inclusive, though it can turn into a popularity contest.

Applause meter

Contestants step forward one at a time and the crowd cheers. Loudest cheer wins. Quick and fun, but hard to judge close calls.

Judges panel

Appoint two or three judges — the host, a senior team member, a kid at a family party. Judges score on creativity, ugliness and presentation. Feels official and adds a little drama.

Combination

Judges pick finalists, then the crowd votes on the winner. Good for large groups.

Make it a runway show

Instead of just voting from the sidelines, give every contestant a moment. Clear a path, play music, and let each person walk the "red carpet." An emcee reads a short description — the sweater's "designer," materials and inspiration (made up, of course). The crowd cheers, and judges take notes.

Fun twists

  • Sweater stories: each contestant shares the origin of their sweater in fifteen seconds.
  • Ugly sweater relay: teams pass one giant sweater down the line, each person putting it on and taking it off.
  • Mystery category: reveal one surprise category at the end.
  • Photo booth: set up a backdrop with props and a frame for photos.
  • Sweater auction: if raising money for charity, have contestants auction off their sweater at the end.

DIY ugly sweater tips

For guests who want to make their own:

  • Start with a plain thrift-store sweater or sweatshirt.
  • Use fabric glue or a hot glue gun (with care) for ornaments, pom-poms and tinsel.
  • Safety pins let you attach and remove items without damaging the sweater.
  • Battery-powered fairy lights can be tucked through knit fabric.
  • Felt is easy to cut into shapes: trees, snowmen, reindeer, letters.

Prize ideas

  • A trophy (spray-paint a plastic one gold)
  • Gift cards
  • A holiday mug filled with candy
  • A homemade crown or sash
  • A printed certificate for each category winner

Certificates are low-cost and people actually keep them — taped to cubicles and fridges long after the party.

Running the contest at an office party

  • Keep it optional and celebrate participants, not just winners.
  • Schedule the contest in the middle of the party, not at the very end when people are leaving.
  • Allow remote team members to join via video with a separate "best on camera" category.
  • Keep humor kind. Costumes should be ugly, not offensive.

Sample timeline

  • Arrival (first 30 minutes): mingling, photos, ballot handout.
  • Runway show (20 minutes): each contestant walks, emcee narrates.
  • Voting (10 minutes): collect ballots during snacks.
  • Awards (15 minutes): announce winners, hand out certificates.
