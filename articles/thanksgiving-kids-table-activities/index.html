Thanksgiving Kids Table Activities That Keep Them Busy

Updated October 14, 2026

The kids table is a Thanksgiving tradition for good reason: the children get their own space, and the adults get to finish a sentence. But a kids table without a plan can turn into a roll-throwing contest by the time the turkey is carved. A little prep goes a long way. Here's how to set up a kids table that keeps children happily occupied from the first course through pie.

Set the table for activity, not just eating

The simplest trick is to treat the kids table as an activity station that happens to have food on it.

  • Cover the table with kraft paper or a cheap paper tablecloth. Kids can draw on it the whole meal. Tape it down at the corners.
  • Put a cup of crayons or washable markers in the middle. Short, chunky crayons work best for little ones; fine markers for older kids.
  • Give each child a place card with their name. Kids love seeing their name, and it heads off seating arguments.
  • Use sturdy, non-breakable dishes. Fewer spills turn into emergencies.

Activities for ages 3 to 6

Gratitude turkey

Trace each child's hand on paper to make a turkey. Each finger "feather" gets something they're thankful for — younger kids can draw it, and an older cousin or adult can write the words. Collect the turkeys and display them on the fridge or the adult table.

Color-by-shape placemats

Simple coloring sheets with big shapes (pumpkins, leaves, pies) keep preschoolers busy. Keep a few spares; there's always a smudge or a spill.

"I Spy" at the table

Little kids love playing I Spy with things on the table — "something orange," "something that's round." It's a calm game that can be played between bites.

Activities for ages 7 to 10

Thanksgiving mystery

Kids this age love a puzzle with a story. Set up a simple mystery: the pie has gone missing, and the kids need to find the culprit using clues. You can make clues yourself (riddles hidden under plates, a code to crack on a napkin) or use a ready-made option. Table trivia

Write a handful of trivia questions on index cards — about fall, food, family history ("What city was Grandma born in?"). Kids take turns reading and answering. Family-history questions are a nice way to get kids curious about relatives at the big table.

Word search and word scramble

Easy to make: write fall and Thanksgiving words (gravy, harvest, pumpkin, cranberry) scrambled on a sheet. First to unscramble them all wins.

Activities for tweens (11 and up)

Tweens can be tricky — too old for crayons, too young to sit through adult conversation. Give them responsibility and a little competition.

  • Make them the table hosts. They can serve drinks to the younger kids, lead a game, or keep score.
  • Run a "best dish" judging panel. Hand them scorecards and let them rate each dish on taste, presentation and creativity. Report results at dessert.
  • Leftover chef challenge. Ask them to design the best leftover sandwich and present it the next day. If you're planning around leftovers anyway, our Leftover Lab planner and container labels has a section for recipe ideas they can fill in.

Games for the whole kids table

Thankful pass-around

Each child says one thing they're thankful for, then passes a "talking spoon" (any spoon will do). The rule: only the spoon holder can talk. It's part gratitude, part crowd control.

Pilgrim, turkey, pie

A seated version of rock-paper-scissors. Pilgrim catches turkey, turkey eats pie (just go with it), pie fills up the pilgrim. Kids invent hand signs for each. Play tournament style.

Story chain

One child starts: "On Thanksgiving morning, the turkey woke up and…" Each kid adds a sentence. Write it down on the paper tablecloth to read aloud at dessert.

Keep the peace with a few rules

  • Assign a "kids table captain." An older cousin or a willing teen can keep things on track and earn first pick at dessert.
  • Seat siblings apart if they tend to squabble. Mix ages so older kids help younger ones.
  • Serve the kids first. Hungry kids get restless fast. Start them with a small plate as soon as possible.
  • Plan a movement break. After the main course, send everyone outside for ten minutes or do a quick dance party before pie.

Prep checklist

  • Paper tablecloth and tape
  • Crayons, markers or colored pencils
  • Place cards
  • Two or three printed activities
  • A small prize bag (stickers, candy, small toys)
  • Wipes and extra napkins
